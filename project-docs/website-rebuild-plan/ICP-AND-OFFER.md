# Cassady Tech Solutions ICP and Relationship

## Ideal customer profile

### Firmographic target

Cassady Tech Solutions should prioritize organizations with:

- 5-50 employees
- A real operating business or established nonprofit mission
- Dependence on email, Microsoft 365, cloud applications, endpoints, internet connectivity, and business data
- No full-time internal IT department, or an internal generalist who needs a strategic partner
- An owner, executive, office manager, or operations leader who can approve changes
- Enough recurring technology need to justify an ongoing relationship

Employee count is a qualification signal, not an absolute rule. A smaller organization may still be an excellent fit when technology is operationally important. A larger organization may be a poor fit when it expects enterprise scale, 24/7 coverage, or capabilities outside the current delivery model.

## Most valuable customer characteristics

The strongest early customers are likely to:

- Feel the cost of downtime, account problems, poor documentation, or technology confusion
- Have recurring technology decisions rather than only one-time repair needs
- Value responsiveness and plain-language guidance
- Need help connecting tools and improving workflows
- Be open to Microsoft 365, automation, and AI adoption
- Have enough operational complexity to benefit from proactive management
- Prefer one accountable technology relationship over several disconnected vendors

## Priority vertical signals

The first website should remain broadly applicable while speaking naturally to:

- Professional services
- Small offices
- Local contractors and trades
- Nonprofits
- Community organizations
- Health-adjacent offices where scope and privacy boundaries are clear
- Legal, accounting, real estate, and other information-dependent businesses

Vertical specialization should be earned through repeated customer work, not assumed in the initial copy.

## Relationship to sell

The core relationship is:

> A practical technology partner for small organizations that need dependable operations, safer systems, and a thoughtful guide for modernization.

This is stronger than selling isolated support hours because the customer is buying continuity, judgment, and reduced technology burden.

## Offer structure

### 1. Technology foundation

A baseline assessment of users, devices, accounts, Microsoft 365, backups, vendors, network, documentation, and obvious operational risks.

Possible deliverables:

- Current-state inventory
- Priority risk and improvement list
- Account and identity review
- Backup and recovery review
- Technology roadmap
- Recommended next actions

### 2. Managed technology operations

Recurring support and oversight sized for a small organization.

Potential components:

- Endpoint and patch oversight
- Account and Microsoft 365 administration
- Security baseline improvements
- Backup oversight
- Remote support
- Vendor coordination
- Documentation
- Recurring technology review

### 3. AI and automation enablement

A practical, security-aware approach to deciding where AI and automation can help.

Potential components:

- Workflow discovery
- Use-case selection
- Tool evaluation
- Safe configuration
- Integrations
- Prompt and process design
- Staff enablement
- Data handling and governance guidance
- Ongoing review

### 4. Projects and advisory work

Separate project engagements for migrations, implementations, workflow redesign, recovery planning, documentation, and other defined outcomes.

## Website conversion goal

The primary conversion should be a **Technology Review** or **Technology and AI Readiness Conversation**, not a generic “contact us” request.

The CTA should invite a conversation about:

- What currently causes friction
- What technology the organization depends on
- What risks or gaps are creating concern
- What the organization wants to improve next
