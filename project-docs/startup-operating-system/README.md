# CTS and GHV Startup Operating System

This folder is the practical operating manual for building Cassady Tech Solutions and Greenhorn Valley Tech without turning the startup into an uncontrolled second job.

## What this is

This is a living execution system for:

- Cassady Tech Solutions: technology for organizations
- Greenhorn Valley Tech: technology for people and homes
- Samuel Cassady: Owner and Technical Director
- Katherine Cassady: proposed Director of Business Operations
- Junior Pulido: limited marketing support when appropriate

The business is currently lean, self-funded, and in a validation and repositioning phase. The goal is not to perform every future capability immediately. The goal is to establish a repeatable rhythm, validate the right customer relationship, and grow only when the business can support the promise.

## Start here

1. Read SAM-STARTUP-GUIDE.md once without trying to complete every task.
2. Read KATHERINE-STARTUP-GUIDE.md together before assigning responsibilities.
3. Use DASHBOARD-AND-TEMPLATES.md to run the weekly and monthly rhythm.
4. Choose only one primary business milestone at a time.
5. Record decisions and open questions in the existing website rebuild planning documents.

## Operating principles

- Protect family finances and family time.
- One meaningful outcome is better than ten unfinished tasks.
- Validate before investing.
- Sell the relationship and outcome, not a tool list.
- Keep CTS and GHV distinct while sharing one practical philosophy.
- Do not promise coverage, security, AI capability, or response times that cannot be delivered.
- Human review remains required for important customer-facing, technical, financial, legal, and security decisions.
- The website strategy remains planning documentation until explicitly authorized for rebuild or deployment.

## Difficulty and time scale

| Level | Meaning | Typical time | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Quick win | 15 to 30 minutes | Follow up with a lead, update a record, send a thank-you |
| L2 | Foundational task | 30 to 90 minutes | Draft an offer, document a process, conduct a discovery call |
| L3 | Deep work | 90 minutes to 3 hours | Build a proposal, analyze competitors, design an onboarding process |
| L4 | High-risk or strategic | 2 to 6 hours | Change pricing, sign a major agreement, accept a complex client, choose a production tool |

L4 work requires Samuel's approval and should not be started casually.

## The minimum viable weekly rhythm

- Samuel: three focused execution blocks, one sales or relationship block, and one weekly review.
- Katherine: Monday planning, one or two follow-up blocks, one documentation or administration block, and Friday closeout.
- Both: one short weekly conversation focused on decisions, capacity, money, and next actions.
- Monthly: review revenue, expenses, pipeline, workload, customer evidence, and whether the current plan still makes sense.

## Definition of progress

A week counts as productive when at least one of these happens:

- A qualified conversation occurs.
- A customer problem becomes clearer.
- A proposal or Technology Review moves forward.
- A delivery process becomes easier to repeat.
- A useful document is completed.
- A decision is made and recorded.
- A risk is reduced.
- A relationship is strengthened.

## What not to do

Do not:

- Rebuild both websites because the plan feels exciting.
- Buy tools before a customer need and margin are clear.
- Create service tiers that cannot be delivered consistently.
- Chase every possible customer type.
- Treat family labor as unlimited or free.
- Let an interesting AI idea replace customer discovery.
- Add recurring clients faster than the support model can handle.
- Confuse activity with traction.

## Current strategic anchor

CTS should sell:

> A practical technology partner for small organizations that need dependable operations, safer systems, and a thoughtful guide for modernization.

GHV should sell:

> Make technology at home work the way it should, with a real person available when it does not.

## Review cadence

At the end of each month, ask:

1. What did customers actually ask for?
2. What did customers actually pay for?
3. Which work was profitable or strategically valuable?
4. What consumed time without producing value?
5. What should we stop, continue, or test next?
6. Can the next month be made simpler?

The answer to the last question should usually be yes.
