# Samuel's Founder Startup Guide

## Purpose

This is your day-to-day operating guide for building CTS and GHV while protecting your job, family, money, energy, and technical credibility.

You are not starting from zero. You already have technical experience, cybersecurity experience, business infrastructure, working communication channels, GitHub documentation, service documents, customer history, and evidence of local demand.

Your challenge is turning capability into a small, repeatable business system.

## Your role

You are:

- Owner and Technical Director
- Final decision-maker for technical delivery, security, pricing, contracts, spending, public claims, warranties, risk acceptance, and major customer commitments
- Primary technical service provider
- Business strategist and relationship owner
- Responsible for deciding what the company will not do

You are not required to:

- Personally perform every administrative task
- Launch every service at once
- Accept every lead
- Build a full MSP before validating the first recurring relationship
- Become a full-time employee of your own startup immediately
- Spend family money to make the business look larger than it is

## Your operating rule

Build the next reliable layer.

Do not build the entire future company in one burst.

## Daily workflow

Use this on workdays and lighter days. Adjust the length to your actual energy and family schedule.

### 1. Ten-minute control check

Difficulty: L1  
Time: 10 minutes

Review:

- New customer messages
- Open commitments
- Calendar
- Money or billing items
- Technical incidents
- One decision that is blocking progress

Write down the single most important outcome for the day.

If nothing is urgent, choose the highest-value task connected to the current milestone.

### 2. Twenty-minute relationship block

Difficulty: L1  
Time: 15 to 30 minutes

Complete one relationship action:

- Follow up with a real lead
- Thank a referral source
- Ask a past customer for feedback
- Schedule a discovery conversation
- Send a proposal clarification
- Check on an unresolved customer question
- Contact one carefully selected commercial prospect

This block exists because businesses grow through conversations, not only documentation.

### 3. One focused execution block

Difficulty: L2 or L3  
Time: 30 to 90 minutes

Choose one:

- Build or improve a Technology Review
- Create a proposal
- Perform a customer assessment
- Document a repeatable service
- Improve onboarding
- Review pricing economics
- Test an AI or automation use case
- Complete a defined customer deliverable
- Research one decision that is currently blocking action

Use a timer. Stop when the block ends unless the work is at a safe stopping point.

### 4. Ten-minute closeout

Difficulty: L1  
Time: 10 minutes

Record:

- What was completed
- What remains
- The next physical action
- Time spent
- Any decision or risk discovered

Do not rely on memory.

## A realistic daily target

On a busy workday:

- 10 minutes of control
- 15 to 30 minutes of relationship work
- 30 to 60 minutes of focused execution
- 10 minutes of closeout

Total: approximately 1 to 2 hours, not necessarily continuous.

On a constrained day:

- One relationship action
- One next action recorded

That still counts.

On a day when family or employment needs come first:

- Do not create guilt-based work.
- Record the interruption if it affects a commitment.
- Resume at the next planned block.

## Weekly workflow

### Monday: choose the week's outcome

Time: 20 to 30 minutes  
Difficulty: L2

Choose:

- One primary business outcome
- One customer or relationship outcome
- One operations or documentation outcome
- One thing you will deliberately not do

Example:

- Primary outcome: complete and test the Technology Review offer
- Relationship outcome: conduct two discovery conversations
- Operations outcome: document the discovery notes template
- Not doing: redesigning the website

### Tuesday or Wednesday: execute and learn

Time: 45 to 90 minutes  
Difficulty: L2 or L3

Do the work that creates evidence:

- Talk to a prospective customer
- Deliver a small paid project
- Observe a recurring problem
- Test a workflow
- Compare the time required to the money received
- Ask what the customer expected but did not say

### Thursday: move revenue forward

Time: 30 to 60 minutes  
Difficulty: L1 or L2

Choose one:

- Follow up on an open opportunity
- Send a proposal
- Ask for a decision
- Confirm the next step
- Request a referral
- Convert a completed project into a possible ongoing relationship

### Friday: close the loop

Time: 20 to 30 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Record:

- Revenue received
- Revenue expected
- Hours spent
- Lead status
- Customer status
- Unresolved risks
- One lesson
- Next week's first action

### Weekly owner review questions

1. Did I create customer value or only internal work?
2. Did I have a real conversation with a potential buyer?
3. Did I make any promise that exceeds current capacity?
4. What took longer than expected?
5. What should become a documented process?
6. What should Katherine own next week?
7. What should be stopped?

## Difficulty model for your work

### L1: Keep the machine moving

Examples:

- Lead follow-up
- Calendar coordination
- Sending a thank-you
- Updating a record
- Writing a next action
- Reviewing a small invoice

Success standard: completed and recorded.

### L2: Build a repeatable capability

Examples:

- Discovery call
- Technology Review draft
- Customer onboarding checklist
- Simple service page copy
- Small workflow automation
- Basic competitor comparison

Success standard: another person could understand what happened and repeat it.

### L3: Make a material business decision

Examples:

- Pricing package
- Recurring service scope
- Tool selection
- Customer fit decision
- Proposal for a meaningful project
- Capacity limit

Success standard: written assumptions, time estimate, cost estimate, risk statement, and decision owner.

### L4: Accept meaningful risk

Examples:

- Regulated or sensitive customer data
- Emergency response commitment
- Complex migration
- Major software spend
- Contract exception
- Security responsibility beyond standard scope

Success standard: written scope, approval, risk decision, and a plan for failure.

## Progressive goals

### Stage 0: Establish control

Target: first 7 days  
Difficulty: L1 to L2

Complete:

- Choose the current primary milestone
- Create one shared startup dashboard
- Identify every active lead and open commitment
- Separate CTS and GHV pipeline entries
- Record current business expenses
- Confirm the weekly owner and Katherine check-in
- Stop or defer unnecessary build work

Exit condition:

You can explain what is active, what is paused, who owns it, and what the next action is.

### Stage 1: Make the offer understandable

Target: days 8 to 30  
Difficulty: L2 to L3

Complete:

- Draft the CTS Technology Review
- Define the GHV service menu in plain language
- Identify launch-ready capabilities
- Identify unsupported or not-yet-ready capabilities
- Draft a simple discovery script
- Create a proposal outline
- Decide what evidence is safe to use publicly
- Conduct at least five useful customer or partner conversations

Exit condition:

A prospective customer can understand what you do, who it is for, and what the next conversation is.

### Stage 2: Validate demand

Target: days 31 to 60  
Difficulty: L2 to L3

Complete:

- Conduct structured conversations with likely CTS customers
- Track objections and buying signals
- Test the Technology Review conversation
- Follow up with current and past GHV contacts
- Identify repeatable customer problems
- Compare proposed price to delivery time
- Record why opportunities move forward or stop

Exit condition:

You have evidence of which problem customers value enough to discuss or pay for.

### Stage 3: Deliver and document

Target: days 61 to 90  
Difficulty: L2 to L4

Complete:

- Deliver a small number of carefully selected engagements
- Document before, during, and after steps
- Track actual time and direct costs
- Improve the onboarding and communication process
- Identify customer outcomes
- Ask for feedback
- Decide whether recurring service is appropriate for each customer

Exit condition:

You can deliver the selected offer more consistently than the first time.

### Stage 4: Build recurring revenue carefully

Target: months 4 to 6  
Difficulty: L3 to L4

Complete:

- Select a limited recurring service model
- Define supported tools and boundaries
- Set realistic response expectations
- Build onboarding and recurring review rhythm
- Establish minimum economic requirements
- Cap customer count based on available capacity
- Use early customers to improve the service rather than to justify overextension

Exit condition:

Recurring customers receive a consistent experience and the economics are visible.

### Stage 5: Scale what works

Target: months 7 to 12  
Difficulty: L3 to L4

Consider:

- Formalizing service tiers
- Improving partner relationships
- Adding subcontractor or hiring capacity
- Publishing stronger proof
- Expanding AI and automation services
- Improving customer reporting
- Increasing recurring revenue only when delivery quality is stable

Exit condition:

Growth improves the business instead of merely increasing workload.

## Decision gate before accepting work

Ask:

1. Is the customer a reasonable fit?
2. Is the requested outcome clear?
3. Can I deliver it with current capability?
4. Can I meet the expected timeline?
5. Is sensitive data involved?
6. Is the price worth the time and risk?
7. Does the work help prove the business model?
8. What will I have to stop to do this work?
9. Is the scope written?
10. What happens if something goes wrong?

If the answers are unclear, pause before promising.

## Weekly conversation with Katherine

Keep it short and concrete:

- What moved forward?
- What needs follow-up?
- What is waiting on Samuel?
- What is waiting on a customer?
- What should Katherine own next?
- Is the workload still fair?
- Did the business spend money this week?
- What should be stopped?

This is a business meeting, not a nightly business argument.

## Your red flags

Slow down when:

- You are adding tools faster than customers.
- You are using family money without a written reason.
- You are accepting work because you feel guilty.
- You cannot explain the offer in two sentences.
- You are promising immediate response while unavailable.
- You are avoiding a pricing conversation.
- You are rebuilding instead of selling or learning.
- You are working late because the system is unclear.
- You are hiding workload from Katherine or yourself.

## Definition of founder success

You are succeeding when:

- The business is becoming easier to explain.
- Customers trust the next step.
- Work is documented well enough to repeat.
- Revenue is becoming less random.
- Katherine has meaningful ownership without carrying the business.
- Your technical credibility is protected.
- Family stress is visible and managed.
- You are learning faster than you are expanding.
