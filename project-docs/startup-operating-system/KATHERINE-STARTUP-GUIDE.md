# Katherine's Startup Guide

## A practical invitation to help build the business

This guide is not a demand that you become a technical expert, salesperson, or full-time employee.

It is a starting point for a meaningful, manageable role in helping Cassady Tech Solutions and Greenhorn Valley Tech become organized, visible, and capable of producing reliable revenue.

The role should strengthen the family, not quietly consume it.

Before this becomes official, you and Samuel should sit down together, discuss the guide, change anything that does not feel fair, and agree on a small first-month experiment.

## The business in plain language

Cassady Tech Solutions serves organizations.

Greenhorn Valley Tech serves people and homes.

They share ownership, values, and technical capability, but they answer different customer questions:

- CTS: Can you help my organization operate and improve through technology?
- GHV: Can you help me make technology work in my home?

Samuel owns technical delivery and final business decisions. You help create order, follow-through, communication, and momentum.

## Your possible role

Working title: Director of Business Operations

Expected starting commitment: approximately 5 to 10 flexible hours per week

The role may be paused, reduced, changed, or ended if family finances, workload, or fairness require it.

Your value is not measured by how busy you look. It is measured by whether your work makes the business easier to operate and easier for customers to trust.

## What you can own

You may own:

- The operations binder and shared business records
- Lead and customer follow-up
- Scheduling and appointment coordination
- Drafting customer messages for approval
- Pipeline updates and next actions
- Basic invoice and payment follow-up
- Marketing coordination
- Partner and referral tracking
- Meeting notes and decision logs
- Keeping the service and offer documents organized
- Preparing questions and recommendations for Samuel
- Tracking what customers ask for
- Maintaining the weekly dashboard
- Helping document useful AI and automation business ideas

## What requires Samuel's approval

Do not independently commit the business to:

- Pricing or discounts
- Contracts or legal terms
- Security guarantees
- Technical promises
- Response-time promises
- Major purchases
- New recurring software
- Work involving sensitive customer data
- Regulated or high-risk customers
- Public claims about capability
- Refunds or exceptions
- A project that could create significant family financial exposure

You can prepare, organize, research, draft, and recommend. Samuel makes the final commitment.

## Your normal weekly rhythm

### Monday planning

Time: 15 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Review:

- New leads
- Existing customers
- Open follow-ups
- Calendar
- Invoices or payment questions
- Samuel's primary business goal
- Your available time

Choose three priorities:

1. One customer or lead action
2. One organization or documentation action
3. One follow-up or revenue action

Time: 10 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Ask:

- What is waiting on someone?
- What is stuck?
- Did a customer need a response?
- Does Samuel need to approve something?
- Is your workload still reasonable?

### Friday closeout

Time: 20 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Record:

- Completed tasks
- Leads contacted
- Responses received
- Meetings scheduled
- Documents updated
- Revenue or invoice information
- Questions for Samuel
- One improvement for next week

### Monthly review

Time: 30 to 45 minutes  
Difficulty: L2

Discuss:

- Revenue
- Expenses
- Hours worked
- Pipeline
- Customer feedback
- Workload
- Family impact
- Compensation
- What to stop, continue, and test

## Your daily workflow

You do not need to work every day. When you do work, use this sequence.

### 1. Open the dashboard

Time: 5 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Look for:

- Leads without a next action
- Customers waiting for a response
- Messages that need Samuel's approval
- Appointments
- Unpaid or unclear invoices
- One useful task that can be completed today

### 2. Complete one follow-up

Time: 10 to 20 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Examples:

- Confirm an appointment
- Thank a referral source
- Ask whether a customer still needs help
- Send a draft message for approval
- Confirm what information is needed
- Update a lead status
- Close the loop on an unanswered question

### 3. Complete one organization task

Time: 20 to 45 minutes  
Difficulty: L1 or L2

Examples:

- Update a pipeline entry
- File a document
- Create meeting notes
- Add a next action
- Update an expense record
- Organize a service description
- Add a customer question to the research log

### 4. Close out

Time: 5 minutes  
Difficulty: L1

Write down what happened and what should happen next.

## Difficulty guide

### Level 1: Simple coordination

15 to 30 minutes.

Examples:

- Scheduling
- Follow-up
- Filing
- Updating a record
- Sending an approved message
- Taking notes

### Level 2: Useful organization

30 to 90 minutes.

Examples:

- Building a lead list
- Creating a partner tracker
- Drafting a customer FAQ
- Organizing a service folder
- Summarizing customer feedback
- Creating a weekly dashboard

### Level 3: Research and recommendation

60 to 120 minutes.

Examples:

- Comparing local competitors
- Researching a partner opportunity
- Drafting a marketing campaign
- Summarizing repeated customer problems
- Preparing a recommendation for Samuel

Level 3 work ends with a recommendation, not an independent commitment.

## First-month plan

### Week 1: Orientation and control

Goal: understand the role and create visibility.

Complete:

- Read this guide with Samuel
- Agree on the time boundary
- Agree on the approval boundary
- Review CTS and GHV differences
- List all current leads and customers
- List open commitments
- Review the shared folders and current documents
- Start a simple time log
- Choose the weekly meeting times

Definition of done:

You know what you own, what Samuel owns, and where the current work is recorded.

### Week 2: Offer and audience

Goal: understand what the business is actually trying to sell.

Complete:

- Review the CTS and GHV service descriptions
- Write a plain-language explanation of each brand
- Identify the most common customer questions
- Create or update a lead qualification list
- Mark each lead as active, waiting, future, no-fit, or closed
- Prepare five approved outreach messages for Samuel's review
- Identify ten qualified prospects or partners over time, not all in one day

Definition of done:

You can explain the difference between CTS and GHV without using technical jargon.

### Week 3: Revenue support

Goal: help real opportunities move.

Complete:

- Follow up with active GHV inquiries
- Identify commercial conversations that need a next action
- Prepare meeting notes
- Track proposals and decisions
- Confirm appointments
- Help Samuel keep promises visible
- Record common objections and questions
- Identify whether any completed work could lead to repeat support

Definition of done:

Every active opportunity has an owner and a next action.

### Week 4: Review and improve

Goal: make the work easier to repeat.

Complete:

- Review hours worked
- Review completed tasks
- Count contacts and follow-ups
- Count opportunities with next actions
- Identify the three most useful documents
- Identify what felt confusing or unfair
- Recommend one process improvement
- Decide whether to continue, reduce, or change the role

Definition of done:

The role feels understandable, useful, and sustainable.

## Your dashboard targets

Initial targets are modest:

- 10 qualified prospects or partners identified over the first month
- 5 approved outreach conversations
- 5 pipeline opportunities with documented next actions
- 5 useful operating documents or updates
- 100 percent of active leads have a status
- 100 percent of scheduled appointments have a next step
- Weekly time log completed

These are learning targets, not quotas.

## Approved communication pattern

When a customer or lead asks for something that is already within an approved service description:

1. Acknowledge the request.
2. Capture the relevant details.
3. Confirm the next step.
4. Avoid promising price, timing, security, or technical outcome unless already approved.
5. Send the details to Samuel when a decision is needed.

A safe response is:

> Thanks for reaching out. I have your request noted and will confirm the next step with Sam. We will get back to you with the appropriate timing and details.

## Customer communication checklist

Before sending:

- Is the message clear?
- Is it respectful?
- Did I answer the question I can answer?
- Did I accidentally promise a price or timeline?
- Does Samuel need to approve it?
- Is private or sensitive information included?
- Did I record the next action?

## What success looks like

You are succeeding when:

- Leads do not disappear.
- Customers receive clear follow-up.
- Samuel knows what needs his attention.
- The business has organized records.
- Documents can be found.
- Repeated work becomes easier.
- You have a voice in improving operations.
- The business does not consume every evening.
- You and Samuel can discuss the business without guessing.

## What this role is not

This role is not:

- A requirement to learn cybersecurity engineering immediately
- Permission to spend family or company money without approval
- A promise of a full-time salary before revenue supports it
- A substitute for a CPA, attorney, payroll provider, or licensed specialist
- A requirement to be available constantly
- A measure of your worth as a spouse
- A reason to sacrifice family needs without discussion

## Boundaries that protect the family

Use these rules:

- Set a weekly hour limit.
- Log the hours honestly.
- Keep business meetings scheduled when possible.
- Do not turn every dinner into a business meeting.
- Discuss money openly.
- Pause work when family needs require it.
- Review whether the role still feels fair.
- Do not treat unpaid time as invisible.
- Do not accept responsibility without authority or information.

## A good first conversation with Samuel

Discuss:

1. What part of the business sounds interesting to you?
2. What part sounds stressful or unclear?
3. What hours feel realistic?
4. What work would you enjoy owning?
5. What work do you not want?
6. What decisions should always come back to Samuel?
7. How should business disagreements be handled?
8. What would make the role feel motivating?
9. What would make the role feel unfair?
10. When should the arrangement be reviewed?

There is no requirement to agree to the whole guide. The first agreement can be a small 30-day experiment.

## Your first next action

Read this with Samuel, mark what feels accurate, mark what needs to change, and choose only the first week's tasks.

The goal is not to become the company overnight.

The goal is to help create order, trust, and momentum.
